What it is

A browser 360 viewer typically cycles through photographs captured at regular angles around an object. Pointer movement or touch selects a frame to suggest rotation; it is a sequence viewer rather than a true 3D model.

When it may help

Product previews, portfolio objects, component inspection and teaching demonstrations can benefit from a closer look without requiring a 3D asset.

What you need

A consistent image sequence of the same subject, captured from a fixed position, plus a browser implementation that maps pointer or touch position to frame index.

How to approach it

  1. 01

    Place the subject on a stable turntable and lock camera position and exposure.

  2. 02

    Capture evenly spaced views around the complete rotation.

  3. 03

    Resize and name frames in a consistent numeric sequence.

  4. 04

    Connect drag and touch movement to frame selection, with keyboard alternatives.

Limitations to keep in mind

No viewer is implemented in this starter. Image count, lighting variation, camera movement and large source files can cause visible jumps or slow loading. Frame rotation is not volumetric 3D.

Common questions

How many frames are needed?

The right count depends on the subject and desired smoothness; consistent spacing matters as much as frame count.

Can it show the back and inside of an object?

It can reveal photographed exterior angles only. Interior views require additional imagery or a different model.
