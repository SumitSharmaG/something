What it is

Tic-tac-toe uses a 3×3 board and alternating marks. A computer opponent can choose moves with a minimax search, which evaluates possible future positions; the project is currently a guide, not a playable board.

When it may help

A compact game for learning turn-based state, win detection, accessible grid interaction and a small adversarial search algorithm.

What you need

A modern browser. A future implementation needs board state, legal-move validation, draw and win checks, an opponent strategy, and keyboard/screen-reader support.

How to approach it

  1. 01

    Choose a mark and start a fresh board in the completed app.

  2. 02

    Select an empty square; the app should reject occupied squares.

  3. 03

    Take turns until a row, column or diagonal is complete, or the board is full.

  4. 04

    Restart to play again and review how the opponent chooses moves.

Limitations to keep in mind

No game or AI runs in this workspace placeholder. An optimal opponent can force a draw when both players play perfectly; difficulty settings need distinct documented strategies.

Common questions

Is the AI available to play now?

No. This static workspace is a planning shell; it does not implement board actions.

Can an AI always win?

Against perfect play, tic-tac-toe has a draw strategy. A strong opponent cannot guarantee a win against perfect defence.
